## What counts as hazardous waste
The rules define hazardous waste by its characteristics, such as toxicity, flammability, corrosivity and reactivity, and list specific waste streams and concentration limits in their schedules. Examples include used oil, spent solvents, sludge from some industrial processes and discarded lead-acid batteries.

## Duties of a waste generator
A generator must get authorisation from the State Pollution Control Board, store the waste safely, keep records, send it only to authorised recyclers or disposal facilities, and file an annual return. Movement of waste off site is tracked using a manifest that follows the consignment. The rules also expect generators to reduce waste at source and to reuse or recycle where possible.

## Treatment and disposal
Waste that cannot be recycled goes to authorised treatment, storage and disposal facilities (TSDFs). These include secure landfills and incinerators that meet design and operating standards.

## Imports and exports
Import and export of hazardous waste is controlled, and some wastes are prohibited. These controls follow India's obligations under the Basel Convention.

## Check the current text
Schedules, forms and procedures are amended from time to time. Check the current consolidated rules before preparing any filing.

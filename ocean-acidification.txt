## The chemistry
The ocean takes up a large share of the carbon dioxide that people release, roughly a quarter according to commonly cited estimates. When carbon dioxide dissolves in seawater it forms carbonic acid, which releases hydrogen ions and lowers the pH. It also reduces the amount of carbonate ions that organisms use to build shells and skeletons of calcium carbonate.

## Scale of change
Surface ocean pH has fallen by about 0.1 units since the start of the industrial era. Because the pH scale is logarithmic, this is an increase of around 30 per cent in acidity. The ocean is still slightly alkaline overall, so the term describes a shift towards lower pH, not a change to an acid.

## Effects on marine life
Corals, molluscs, some plankton and other organisms that build calcium carbonate structures find it harder to do so as carbonate availability falls. Effects on the growth, behaviour and survival of fish and other species are also being studied. Ocean acidification is a separate problem from marine heatwaves and coral bleaching, although they often act together on the same reefs.

## Response
The main remedy is to cut carbon dioxide emissions (see [[climate-change]]). Local measures such as reducing pollution and overfishing can help ecosystems cope while emissions are reduced.

How Indian Businesses Can Reduce Their Carbon Footprint in 2025

By BEC Experts  |  6 min read  |  May 2025

Reducing carbon emissions is no longer optional for Indian businesses — it is a strategic imperative driven by regulation, investor expectations, and customer demand. The good news is that many carbon reduction measures also cut operational costs significantly.

🌱 Companies that proactively manage their carbon footprint are 21% more profitable on average than those that don't, according to CDP research.

Step-by-Step: How to Reduce Your Carbon Footprint

  • 1
    Conduct a Carbon Footprint Assessment Measure your Scope 1 (direct), Scope 2 (electricity), and Scope 3 (supply chain) emissions. You cannot manage what you don't measure.
  • 2
    Perform an Energy Audit Identify where energy is being wasted in your operations. Most businesses can reduce energy consumption by 15–30% through efficiency measures alone.
  • 3
    Switch to Renewable Energy Install rooftop solar, sign a Power Purchase Agreement (PPA) with a renewable energy provider, or purchase Renewable Energy Certificates (RECs).
  • 4
    Green Your Supply Chain Engage suppliers on their emissions, prefer local sourcing to reduce transport emissions, and set supplier sustainability standards.
  • 5
    Set Science-Based Targets Commit to emission reduction targets aligned with the Paris Agreement using the Science Based Targets initiative (SBTi) framework.
  • 6
    Offset Remaining Emissions For emissions that cannot yet be eliminated, invest in high-quality carbon offsets such as reforestation, clean cookstoves, or renewable energy projects in India.

The Business Case for Carbon Reduction

Beyond environmental benefits, reducing carbon emissions delivers tangible business returns. Lower energy bills, improved supply chain resilience, access to green financing at lower interest rates, and stronger relationships with ESG-conscious investors and clients all contribute to the bottom line.

Indian exporters also face growing pressure from the EU Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism (CBAM), which will impose carbon costs on imports into Europe from 2026. Companies that have already reduced emissions will be better placed to compete.

Plastic pollution

Plastic pollution is the build-up of plastic waste in land, rivers and oceans. Plastic breaks down very slowly, and it fragments into microplastics that spread through water, soil, air and food chains.

The problem

Plastics are cheap, light and durable, which is why they are used so widely, especially in packaging. The same durability means they persist for a very long time. Plastic enters the environment through littering, poorly managed waste, drains and rivers, fishing gear, the wear of tyres and the shedding of synthetic textiles.

Harm

Animals can be injured by entanglement or by swallowing plastic. Waste can block drains and add to urban flooding. Plastics also contain additives, and fragments can carry other pollutants. Microplastics have been found in oceans, soils, drinking water and food. Research into their effects on human health is continuing.

Policy in India

India regulates plastic through the Plastic Waste Management Rules, 2016, as amended. Identified single-use plastic items were banned from 1 July 2022. Producers, importers and brand owners also have responsibilities under extended producer responsibility (see Extended Producer Responsibility (India)).

Global negotiations

In 2022, the UN Environment Assembly agreed to start negotiating an international legally binding instrument on plastic pollution, covering the full life cycle of plastics. Negotiating sessions have continued since. The status of the talks changes, so check the UNEP website for the latest position.

Approaches

Common approaches are reducing unnecessary plastic use, redesigning products and packaging, reuse systems, better collection and recycling, and ending leakage to the environment (see Circular economy).

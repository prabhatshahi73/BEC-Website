## The three scopes
- **Scope 1** covers direct emissions from sources the organisation owns or controls. Examples are fuel burned in boilers and furnaces, fuel used in company vehicles, process emissions and leaks of refrigerant gases.
- **Scope 2** covers indirect emissions from the generation of purchased electricity, steam, heating and cooling that the organisation uses.
- **Scope 3** covers all other indirect emissions in the organisation's value chain, both upstream and downstream. The standard groups them into 15 categories, such as purchased goods and services, business travel, employee commuting, transport and the use of sold products.

## Gases and units
The GHG Protocol covers the seven greenhouse gases of the Kyoto framework: carbon dioxide, methane, nitrous oxide, hydrofluorocarbons, perfluorocarbons, sulphur hexafluoride and nitrogen trifluoride. Each gas is converted to carbon dioxide equivalent (CO2e) using its global warming potential, so that different gases can be added together.

## Two ways to count Scope 2
The Scope 2 guidance asks for two figures. The location-based method uses the average emission factor of the electricity grid where the energy is used. The market-based method reflects the electricity the organisation has chosen to buy, for example under a renewable energy contract.

## Why it matters
Scope 3 is often the largest share of an organisation's footprint but is the hardest to measure, because it depends on data from suppliers and customers. Buyers, lenders and regulators increasingly ask for these figures. In India, the BRSR framework asks listed companies to disclose Scope 1 and Scope 2 emissions (see [[business-responsibility-and-sustainability-reporting]]).

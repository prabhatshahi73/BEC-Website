Oceans, rivers, lakes, wildlife sanctuaries and canals in one place. Pick a category, filter by letter or search, then click any entry to open its facts.

Oceans and seas

Andaman SeaIndia, Myanmar, Thailand, Indonesia

A marginal sea of the Indian Ocean east of the Andaman and Nicobar Islands, with depths of more than 4,000 m.

Arabian SeaIndia, Pakistan, Oman, Yemen, Somalia

About 3.9 million km², with a maximum depth near 4,600 m. Its winds and temperatures drive the Indian monsoon.

Arctic OceanArctic

About 14.1 million km². The smallest and shallowest ocean, largely covered by sea ice and bordered by Russia, Norway, Greenland, Canada and the United States.

Atlantic OceanAmericas, Europe, Africa

About 106 million km². Split by the Mid-Atlantic Ridge; its deepest point, in the Puerto Rico Trench, is roughly 8,400 m.

Baltic SeaNorthern Europe

About 377,000 km². A shallow, brackish sea with limited exchange with the North Sea, so it is sensitive to nutrient pollution.

Bay of BengalIndia, Bangladesh, Myanmar, Sri Lanka

Roughly 2.2 million km², the largest bay in the world. It receives the Ganga, Brahmaputra, Irrawaddy and many other rivers.

Bering SeaUSA (Alaska), Russia

About 2.3 million km², linking the Pacific and the Arctic through the Bering Strait; major commercial fisheries.

Black SeaEurope, Turkey, Caucasus

About 436,000 km² and up to about 2,200 m deep. Its deep waters contain almost no oxygen.

Caribbean SeaCentral America, Caribbean, northern South America

About 2.75 million km², a warm sea with extensive coral reefs, mangroves and seagrass beds.

Coral SeaAustralia, Papua New Guinea, Pacific islands

Lies off north-eastern Australia and includes the waters of the Great Barrier Reef.

Indian OceanAsia, Africa, Australia

About 70.6 million km². Drives the South Asian monsoon; deepest point is in the Java Trench, roughly 7,300 m.

Laccadive SeaIndia, Sri Lanka, Maldives

The sea off India’s south-west coast that surrounds Lakshadweep and the Maldives, with coral atolls.

Mediterranean SeaEurope, North Africa, Western Asia

About 2.5 million km², linked to the Atlantic by the Strait of Gibraltar, which is only about 14 km wide at its narrowest.

North SeaNorth-western Europe

About 570,000 km² and shallow, averaging roughly 95 m. A major fishing ground and offshore energy region.

Pacific OceanAsia, Americas, Oceania

About 165 million km². The largest and deepest ocean; Challenger Deep in the Mariana Trench is roughly 10,900 m deep.

Persian GulfIran, Gulf Arab states, Iraq

About 250,000 km² and shallow, averaging around 50 m. Very saline and warm, and central to global oil shipping.

Red SeaEgypt, Saudi Arabia, Sudan, Eritrea, Yemen

About 2,250 km long and linked to the Mediterranean by the Suez Canal. Known for coral reefs that tolerate warm water.

Sargasso SeaNorth Atlantic

Defined by ocean currents rather than coastlines, and named for the floating Sargassum seaweed that gathers there.

Sea of JapanJapan, Korea, Russia

A nearly enclosed marginal sea of the Pacific with a maximum depth of about 3,700 m.

South China SeaChina, Southeast Asia

About 3.5 million km², one of the busiest shipping routes in the world; maritime boundaries are disputed.

Southern OceanAntarctica

About 21.9 million km². Circles Antarctica and carries the Antarctic Circumpolar Current. Treated as a distinct ocean by the IHO since 2000 and by National Geographic since 2021.

Tasman SeaAustralia, New Zealand

The part of the south-west Pacific between Australia and New Zealand.

Rivers

Amazon RiverSouth America

About 6,400 km. Carries the largest discharge of any river, around 209,000 m³/s.

BrahmaputraIndia, China, Bangladesh

About 2,900 km in total. Rises in Tibet as the Yarlung Tsangpo and flows through Arunachal Pradesh and Assam before joining the Ganga delta.

ChambalIndia

About 960 km. A Yamuna tributary through Madhya Pradesh, Rajasthan and Uttar Pradesh, known for its ravines and a protected gharial sanctuary.

Congo RiverCentral Africa

About 4,700 km. The deepest known river, with depths beyond 220 m.

DanubeEurope

About 2,850 km. Flows through or along the border of ten countries to the Black Sea.

Ganga (Ganges)India, Bangladesh

About 2,525 km. Rises at Gangotri and reaches the Bay of Bengal, forming the Sundarbans delta with the Brahmaputra.

GodavariIndia

About 1,465 km. The longest river of peninsular India and the second longest in India after the Ganga.

IndusPakistan, India, China

About 3,180 km in total, flowing from Tibet through Ladakh and Pakistan to the Arabian Sea. Its basin supports the Indus irrigation system.

Kaveri (Cauvery)India

About 800 km from the Western Ghats in Karnataka to the Bay of Bengal in Tamil Nadu; the subject of a long-running water-sharing dispute.

KrishnaIndia

About 1,400 km from the Western Ghats in Maharashtra through Karnataka, Telangana and Andhra Pradesh to the Bay of Bengal.

MahanadiIndia (Chhattisgarh, Odisha)

About 858 km. Rises in Chhattisgarh and is dammed at Hirakud before reaching the Bay of Bengal.

MekongSoutheast Asia

About 4,350 km. Flows from the Tibetan Plateau through six countries to Vietnam.

Mississippi–MissouriNorth America

About 6,275 km combined. The main river system of the United States, draining into the Gulf of Mexico.

NarmadaIndia

About 1,312 km, flowing west through Madhya Pradesh, Maharashtra and Gujarat to the Arabian Sea, unlike most peninsular rivers.

NileAfrica

About 6,650 km. Often cited as the longest river; its basin covers eleven countries. Length versus the Amazon is debated.

TaptiIndia

About 724 km, flowing west through Madhya Pradesh, Maharashtra and Gujarat to the Arabian Sea.

VolgaRussia

About 3,530 km. The longest river in Europe, flowing into the Caspian Sea.

YamunaIndia

About 1,376 km. The largest tributary of the Ganga, rising at Yamunotri and joining it at Prayagraj.

YangtzeChina

About 6,300 km. The longest river in Asia, flowing entirely within China.

Lakes

Caspian SeaCentral Asia, Europe

About 371,000 km². The largest inland body of water; salinity is roughly a third of ocean water.

Chilika LakeIndia (Odisha)

A large brackish-water lagoon on the Bay of Bengal coast and a Ramsar wetland, known for wintering waterbirds.

Dead SeaIsrael, Jordan, Palestine

Shoreline about 430 m below sea level, the lowest land elevation on Earth. Hypersaline and shrinking.

Lake BaikalRussia

Deepest lake at about 1,642 m and among the oldest, with roughly a fifth of the world’s unfrozen surface fresh water.

Lake SuperiorUSA, Canada

About 82,100 km². The largest freshwater lake in the world by surface area.

Lake TanganyikaEast and Central Africa

About 1,470 m deep, the second deepest lake, and the longest freshwater lake at roughly 673 km.

Lake TiticacaPeru, Bolivia

At about 3,810 m, the highest large navigable lake in the world.

Lake VictoriaEast Africa

About 68,800 km². Africa’s largest lake and a source of the White Nile.

Wular LakeIndia (Jammu and Kashmir)

One of Asia’s largest freshwater lakes and a Ramsar wetland.

Wildlife sanctuaries and protected areas

Achanakmar Wildlife SanctuaryIndia (Chhattisgarh)

Part of the Achanakmar–Amarkantak Biosphere Reserve; forest habitat that supports tigers and leopards.

Galápagos IslandsEcuador

World Heritage Site since 1978, with a marine reserve and endemic species that shaped Darwin’s ideas.

Great Barrier Reef Marine ParkAustralia

About 344,000 km². The largest coral reef system in the world, a World Heritage Site since 1981.

Kaziranga National ParkIndia (Assam)

Home to most of the world’s greater one-horned rhinoceroses; World Heritage Site since 1985.

Keoladeo National ParkIndia (Rajasthan)

Bharatpur’s wetland reserve; a Ramsar site and World Heritage Site famous for migratory waterbirds.

Kruger National ParkSouth Africa

About 19,500 km². A national park since 1926 and a stronghold for the Big Five.

Ranthambore National ParkIndia (Rajasthan)

A tiger reserve of dry deciduous forest around a medieval fort.

SerengetiTanzania

Site of the Great Migration, with well over a million wildebeest moving with the rains.

SundarbansIndia, Bangladesh

The largest mangrove forest in the world and a refuge of the Bengal tiger; World Heritage Site.

Yellowstone National ParkUSA

Established in 1872 and widely regarded as the first national park in the world.

Canals

Canal du MidiFrance

About 240 km, completed in 1681, linking the Atlantic and the Mediterranean; World Heritage Site since 1996.

Corinth CanalGreece

About 6.3 km, opened in 1893, cut through rock only about 21 m wide.

Erie CanalUSA

Opened in 1825, about 584 km from the Hudson River to Lake Erie.

Ganga Canal (Upper)India

Opened in 1854 to irrigate the Ganga–Yamuna doab from Haridwar.

Grand CanalChina

About 1,776 km from Beijing to Hangzhou, the longest artificial waterway in the world.

Indira Gandhi CanalIndia (Rajasthan)

A canal system of roughly 650 km that carries Himalayan river water into the Thar Desert.

Kiel CanalGermany

About 98 km, opened in 1895, joining the North Sea and the Baltic; one of the busiest artificial waterways.

Panama CanalPanama

About 82 km, opened in 1914 and widened in 2016, using locks to cross the isthmus.

Suez CanalEgypt

About 193 km, opened in 1869, joining the Mediterranean and the Red Sea.

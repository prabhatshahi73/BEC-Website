## The idea
Under EPR, the cost and duty of collecting and recycling a product at the end of its life sit with the company that put it on the market, instead of with municipalities and the public. The aim is to raise collection and recycling rates and to encourage products that are easier to recycle.

## Who is covered
Rules refer to producers, importers and brand owners, often shortened to PIBOs. Depending on the rule, recyclers, refurbishers and manufacturers of covered goods also have duties. Which entities and products are covered, and the size of the duty, differ from rule to rule.

## How it works in India
Covered entities must register on an online portal run by the Central Pollution Control Board. They are given targets for collecting, recycling or reusing a share of what they place on the market. They can meet targets through authorised recyclers or other registered entities, and the work is documented through certificates and annual returns. Non-compliance can lead to environmental compensation and other action.

## Keeping up with changes
EPR rules, targets and portal procedures have been amended often since 2016. Check the current notification and the CPCB portal before relying on any figure or deadline.

CECB's IWMMS: An Exemplary Model for Digital Waste Governance

By BEC Experts · 6 min read

India generates an estimated 7 to 8 million tonnes of hazardous industrial waste every year, yet by the Central Pollution Control Board's own assessment, only 25 to 30 percent of it receives proper treatment. The gap between what is generated and what is safely managed has traditionally been a story of manual registers, delayed inspections, and paperwork that is easy to falsify and hard to audit. Against that backdrop, the Chhattisgarh Environment Conservation Board's Industrial Waste Management and Monitoring System — IWMMS — stands out as one of the more genuinely effective digital governance tools to emerge from an Indian State Pollution Control Board in recent years.

What IWMMS Actually Does

Developed in partnership with CSM Technologies and operated by CECB, IWMMS digitises the entire lifecycle of industrial waste — from the moment an industry applies for authorisation, through transport, to final disposal or reuse. Rather than relying on paper manifests and periodic physical inspections that can be manipulated or delayed, the system builds accountability directly into the workflow.

Online NOC Management
Industries self-register and apply for No Objection Certificates digitally; Regional Officers approve or reject through an automated workflow, cutting out paperwork delays.
Geo-fenced Trip Monitoring
Waste transport vehicles operate within defined geo-fenced routes, with GPS tracking ensuring waste actually reaches an authorised disposal or reuse site.
Mobile Verification
An Android app lets loaders, drivers and site personnel upload geo-tagged photographs and update trip status in real time.
Role-Based Dashboards
Industries, contractors, drivers, Regional Officers and CECB officials each get secure, role-specific access to the same verified data set.

For CECB's Regional Officers, the system functions as a centralised command centre — instant visibility into application status, compliance history and key performance indicators across the state, with automated alerts flagging missed trips, anomalies, or unauthorised disposal attempts. This shifts enforcement effort away from routine paperwork checks and toward genuine field verification and risk-based prioritisation.

The Results So Far

More than 100 industries across Chhattisgarh have already been onboarded onto the platform, and early assessments point to measurable gains in transparency and efficiency: fewer illegal dumping incidents, faster NOC turnaround times, and a data trail that regulators can actually query and analyse rather than reconstruct from paper files after the fact. Because every trip is geo-tagged and time-stamped, disputes between industries, transporters and the Board over whether waste was actually delivered to an authorised facility become far easier to resolve conclusively.

Why This Model Deserves Wider Attention

What makes IWMMS notable is not any single feature in isolation — GPS tracking and mobile verification exist elsewhere — but the fact that CECB built a genuinely end-to-end system covering authorisation, transport and disposal under one platform, with role-based access that keeps every stakeholder, including the regulator itself, working off the same verified data. Many state portals stop at digitising applications; far fewer extend real-time oversight all the way through to the disposal site.

What It Means for Industry in Chhattisgarh

For thermal power, mining and manufacturing units operating in the state — including the ash management and hazardous waste streams BEC regularly advises on — IWMMS represents a shift from periodic compliance reporting to continuous, auditable compliance. Units that maintain clean transport and disposal records build a demonstrable track record that supports faster consent renewals and reduces exposure during inspections, while the system's transparency also raises the cost of non-compliance considerably. As other states look to modernise their own waste oversight, Chhattisgarh's IWMMS offers a working template: digitise the whole chain, not just the application form, and put verifiable field data — not paper declarations — at the centre of enforcement.

Source: Chhattisgarh Environment Conservation Board (enviscecb.org), CHiPS project documentation, and CSM Technologies case studies on the IWMMS platform.
